Large-scale formalization projects have long needed tools to
coordinate contributions, track dependencies, and connect mathematical
exposition with formal proofs. A key example is Lean
Blueprint. Introduced in 2020 for the sphere eversion project, Lean
Blueprint extends plasTeX to provide a shared map of statements,
dependencies, Lean declarations, and progress. Lean Blueprint's model
has been hugely influential in shaping how mathematicians and
formalizers work together. Its design and functionality were our
starting point in this project.

This talk presents recent work on Verso Blueprint, a Lean-native
implementation of the Lean Blueprint concept built on Verso, Lean's
official markup language. Designed for close integration with Lean,
Verso Blueprint maintains a three-layer database linking original
source material, detailed informal statements, and their formalized
counterparts. The database is populated from Verso documents, analysis
of Lean declarations, and other user-provided data. Interactive HTML
views provide navigation and previews, connecting mathematical
exposition with the formal development. We have now largely reached
our original goal, with functionality comparable to Lean Blueprint or
Lean Architect.

But matching that functionality is no longer enough. This is an
extraordinary moment for formalization: while we have been building
the tool, the assumptions behind its design have radically changed.

When we began this project, our central use case was for humans to
write detailed statements and dependencies to guide
formalization. Today, coding agents are increasingly writing
blueprints themselves and interacting with them
autonomously. Blueprints are also taking on the reverse role: not just
guiding the construction of formal proofs, but making AI-generated
proofs intelligible to humans. At the same time, dependency tracking
and organizational information remain essential for coordinating
large-scale autoformalization. For example, Prove2Me, a theorem
database with dependency tracking, played a key coordination role in
the recent autoformalization of Fermat's Last Theorem by helping
agents to organize and schedule formalization tasks.

What comes next for blueprints? We are redesigning Verso Blueprint
around the idea of "programmatic blueprints": human-readable,
machine-actionable objects synchronized with formal developments. Our
ambition is for blueprints to become a *foundational harness for
AI-assisted formalization*, maintaining the shared state of a project
and coordinating what happens next across mathematicians, formalizers,
agents, and code repository workflows.
