Large-scale formalization projects have long needed tools to
coordinate contributions, track dependencies, and connect mathematical
exposition with formal proofs. A key example is Lean
Blueprint. Introduced in 2020 for the sphere eversion project, Lean
Blueprint extends plasTeX to provide a shared map of statements,
dependencies, Lean declarations, and progress. Lean Blueprint's model
has been quite influential and has helped shape how mathematicians and
formalizers work together on large-scale formalization projects.

This talk presents recent work on Verso Blueprint, a Lean-native
implementation of the Lean Blueprint concept built on Verso, Lean's
official markup language. Designed for close integration with Lean,
Verso Blueprint maintains a three-layer database linking source
material, informal statements, and their formalized counterparts. The
database is populated from Verso documents, analysis of Lean
declarations, and other user-provided data. Interactive HTML views
provide navigation and previews, connecting mathematical exposition
with the formal development. We have now largely reached our original
goal, with functionality comparable to Lean Blueprint or Lean
Architect.

But matching that functionality is no longer enough: this is an
extraordinary moment for formalisation. The practice is radically
chaining even as we build its tools.

When we began this project, our central use case was for humans to
write detailed statements and dependencies to guide
formalization. Today, coding agents are increasingly writing and
interacting with the blueprints themselves in an autonomous
fashion. Blueprints are taking on the reverse role: not just guiding
the construction of formal proofs, but making AI-generated proofs
intelligible to humans. At the same time, dependency tracking and
organizational information remain essential for coordinating
large-scale autoformalization. For example, Prove2Me, a theorem
database with dependency tracking, played a key coordination role in
the recent Fermat's Last Theorem auto-formalization milestone.

What comes next for blueprints in this extraordinary moment? We are
redesigning Verso Blueprint around the idea of "programmatic
blueprints": human-readable, machine-actionable objects synchronized
with formal developments. Our ambition is for blueprints to become a
*foundational harness for AI-assisted formalization*, maintaining the
shared state of a project and coordinating what happens next across
mathematicians, formalizers, code repository workflows, and agents.
